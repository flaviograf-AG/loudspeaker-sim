LS Graf Simulator — Loudspeaker Enclosure Calculator & Passive/Active Crossover Design CAD

LS Graf Simulator is a free, browser-based loudspeaker design and acoustic simulation application powered by a Rust/WebAssembly physics engine and a verified 1,308-driver Thiele-Small database. It unifies 1-way, 2-way, 3-way, and 4-way speaker cabinet modeling (Sealed, Bass-Reflex/Vented, Passive Radiator, 4th & 6th-Order Bandpass, Quarter-Wave Transmission Line, Multi-Segment Horn, and Open Baffle), passive RLC ladder crossover synthesis, active DSP biquad filtering (including Linkwitz Transform and Nelder-Mead optimization), multi-angle FRD/ZMA measurement import, and 3D cabinet cut-list generation in a single real-time workspace — serving as a modern, cross-platform online alternative to WinISD, VituixCAD, XSim, Boxsim, and Hornresp.

What Loudspeaker Enclosure Types Can You Simulate?

Loudspeaker enclosure design governs low-frequency extension (F3, F6, F10), transient damping (group delay), electrical impedance loading, and mechanical cone excursion vs. Xmax. LS Graf Simulator models seven distinct acoustic alignments across 20 Hz to 20 kHz:

Enclosure Alignment Acoustic Solver Model Key Design Parameters & Readouts
Sealed (Closed Box) 2nd-order acoustic suspension (12 dB/oct roll-off) Internal volume Vb (L), box loss Ql, system resonance Fc = Fs · √(1 + Vas/Vb), total Qtc presets: Bessel (0.577), Butterworth (0.707), Chebyshev (0.957)
Vented (Bass-Reflex) 4th-order Helmholtz resonator (24 dB/oct roll-off) Box volume Vb, port area Sp, port length Lp, Helmholtz tuning Fb, B4 / QB3 / SC4 / EBS alignments, circular & slot ports, organ-pipe resonance f_pipe, port air velocity V_port vs. 17 m/s chuffing threshold
Passive Radiator (PR) 4th-order coupled mass-compliance drone cone Box volume Vb, radiator area Sd(PR), compliance Cms(PR), moving mass Mms(PR), mechanical damping Rms(PR)
4th & 6th-Order Bandpass Single-reflex (sealed rear + vented front) & dual-reflex (vented rear + vented front) Rear chamber volume V_rear, front chamber volume V_front, front and rear port tunings Fb(front) & Fb(rear)
Quarter-Wave Transmission Line 2×2 Acoustic Transfer Matrix Method (TMM) waveguide Line length L (f_λ/4 = c / 4L), driver & mouth cross-sectional areas, Straight/Conical/Exponential taper, driver offset %, 180° folds, multi-zone Delany-Bazley fibrous stuffing
Multi-Segment Horn Cascaded 2×2 TMM horn waveguide + rear chamber Up to 4 segments with Conical, Exponential, Hyperbolic (Salmon T-parameter), or Tractrix flare profiles, throat/mouth areas, flare cutoff fc, and sealed/vented rear chamber
Open Baffle (Dipole) Finite-baffle dipole path-length cancellation Baffle width W, height H, asymmetric driver offset, dipole cancellation frequency

How Does Passive RLC & Active DSP Crossover Simulation Work?

Designing an accurate loudspeaker crossover requires modeling the interaction between filter components, complex driver impedance Z(f), acoustic phase, physical voice-coil depth offset (Z_offset), and cabinet front-baffle diffraction. LS Graf Simulator supports Active DSP, Passive RLC, and Hybrid crossover architectures:

  • Interactive Passive RLC Ladder Network: Build custom passive networks with Series & Shunt Inductors (L with DC resistance DCR), Capacitors (C), Resistors (R), parallel R ∥ C high-frequency bypass networks, Baffle-Step R ∥ L networks (f_bsc = 115 / W_m), parallel-RLC traps (Sperrkreis), series-RLC shunt notches (Saugkreis), Zobel impedance linearization (Rz = 1.25 · Re, Cz = Le / Re²), conjugate Fs resonance notches, and L-Pad attenuators.
  • Standard Filter Targets & Reverse-Null Verification: Compare actual acoustic driver roll-offs against ideal 1st-order Butterworth (6 dB/oct), 2nd-order Butterworth (BW2, 12 dB/oct), 2nd-order Linkwitz-Riley (LR2, 12 dB/oct), and 4th-order Linkwitz-Riley (LR4, 24 dB/oct) target slopes, and run a 1-click Reverse-Null Polarity Test to verify phase coherence at the crossover frequency.
  • Active DSP Biquad & Linkwitz Transform Lab: Apply per-way Parametric EQ (PEQ), Low/High Shelving, 1st/2nd-order All-Pass phase filters, and Linkwitz Transform sealed-box sub-bass extension (f0, Q0 → fp, Qp), then export exact DSP biquad coefficients for miniDSP and Equalizer APO.
  • Multi-Angle FRD & ZMA Measurement Import: Upload on-axis and off-axis (15°, 30°, 45°, 60°) .frd frequency response and .zma impedance files to evaluate off-axis polar directivity, or enable Hybrid Mode to simulate box and port changes on top of measured driver data.
  • 3D Cabinet Cut-List & Passive BOM Generator: Automatically compute MDF or Birch Plywood panel cut-lists (Butt Joint or 45° Miter), internal bracing/driver/port volume displacements, vertical driver spacing vs. crossover wavelength (d_ctc vs. λ), and export a complete Markdown Bill of Materials (BOM) with E12/E24 standard component values.

Frequently Asked Questions (Loudspeaker Enclosure & Crossover Design)

How do I choose between a Sealed and a Vented (Bass-Reflex) enclosure?

Divide the driver's free-air resonance Fs by its electrical Q factor Qes to find the Efficiency Bandwidth Product (EBP = Fs / Qes). Drivers with Qts > 0.45 and EBP < 50 are best suited for Sealed enclosures (where Qtc = 0.707 gives a maximally flat Butterworth response and Qtc = 0.577 gives a transient-optimal Bessel response). Drivers with 0.25 ≤ Qts ≤ 0.42 and EBP > 60 excel in Vented (Bass-Reflex) enclosures using B4, QB3, SC4, or EBS alignments.

Why do textbook passive crossover calculators fail on real loudspeaker drivers?

Textbook LC formulas assume a constant 4 Ω or 8 Ω resistor. Real loudspeaker drivers have rising impedance at high frequencies from voice-coil inductance (Le), a large impedance peak at fundamental resonance (Fs), non-flat acoustic frequency response, and a 4–6 dB baffle-step transition at f_bsc ≈ 115 / W_m. LS Graf Simulator solves the full complex voltage-divider ladder against the driver's real complex impedance Z(f) and provides 1-click Zobel, conjugate Fs notch, Baffle-Step R ∥ L, and L-Pad calculators.
